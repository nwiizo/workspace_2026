# Jiu-Jitsu (BJJ / Grappling) Research Bank

Purpose: cited knowledge bank for designing a teaching 3D BJJ game (questions, continuous flow puzzles, opponent archetypes). Each technical claim links a source. "if-then" chains are flagged as **[FLOW]** because they map directly to game branching. Uncertainty is marked **[UNCERTAIN]**.

Conventions:
- "if-then" = opponent reaction -> your correct next move. These are the core game mechanic.
- Legality and "correct answer" depend on **ruleset + belt** (see Section 9). A move can be the highest-percentage finish in one context and a disqualification in another.
- All URLs collected per-section and in a master list at the bottom.

## Section 1 — Submission Chains / Linking (Gi & No-Gi)

### Core principle
- A chain = primary attack provokes a defensive reaction; that reaction opens the secondary attack; the last-ditch movement opens a tertiary attack. You **follow reactions, you do not force finishes**. ([Evolve University](https://evolve-university.com/blog/basic-bjj-combinations-triangle-choke-omoplata-and-armbar/), [BJJ Graph Submission Chains](https://bjjgraph.org/Principles/Submission-Chains))
- Elite players "follow the reaction": opponent rolls forward -> follow to top; postures up -> switch to sweep; hides the arm -> armbar or back take. ([BJJInfo Submission Chains](https://bjjinfo.org/wiki/Submission_Chains))
- The armbar -> triangle -> omoplata chain is the recommended *beginner* chain (builds timing, flexibility, reaction awareness) -> good first tier in a game. ([Evolve University](https://evolve-university.com/blog/basic-bjj-combinations-triangle-choke-omoplata-and-armbar/))

### Chain A: Armbar <-> Triangle <-> Omoplata (the "holy trinity" from guard/attacking an arm)
**[FLOW]**
- Attack armbar -> opponent **pulls arm free** -> triangle (their arm is now out, head/one-arm-in) . ([BJJ Graph](https://bjjgraph.org/Principles/Submission-Chains))
- In triangle -> opponent **postures up / pushes** -> kimura or switch to armbar; opponent **hides the arm to escape triangle** -> omoplata (hiding the arm exposes the shoulder). The triangle->omoplata link is described as "one of the most reliable chains in BJJ." ([Evolve University](https://evolve-university.com/blog/basic-bjj-combinations-triangle-choke-omoplata-and-armbar/))
- In omoplata -> opponent **rolls forward** to escape -> follow to top / re-attack; opponent **postures up** -> switch to sweep; opponent **hides/straightens the arm** -> transition to armbar or back take. ([BJJInfo](https://bjjinfo.org/wiki/Submission_Chains))
- Omoplata fallback: if no finish, omoplata still **sweeps to side control**. ([Kingz](https://www.kingz.com/blogs/news/how-to-master-the-omoplata-bjj))

### Chain B: Closed-guard Hip-bump <-> Kimura <-> Guillotine <-> Omoplata
**[FLOW]**
- Opponent **postures back too far** -> hip-bump sweep. ([BjjTribes](https://bjjtribes.com/2020/08/14/the-hip-bump-sweep-from-closed-guard/))
- Hip-bump -> opponent **posts the hand / bases out** -> the posting arm is exposed -> kimura. ([BjjTribes](https://bjjtribes.com/2020/08/14/the-hip-bump-sweep-from-closed-guard/))
- Kimura -> opponent **strongly defends (hides hand to belt/stiff-arms)** -> switch to omoplata (legs add power; very hard to defend with strength). ([BjjTribes](https://bjjtribes.com/2020/08/14/the-hip-bump-sweep-from-closed-guard/))
- Kimura defense -> opponent **uses head to force their arm back inside** -> guillotine appears. ([BjjTribes](https://bjjtribes.com/2020/08/14/the-hip-bump-sweep-from-closed-guard/))

### Chain C: Kimura Trap System (control-grip-driven)
**[FLOW]** The figure-four grip is held; opponent's reaction dictates the path. ([Evolve](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/elevate-your-bjj-game-using-the-kimura-trap/), [RollBliss](https://rollbliss.com/blogs/news/kimura-trap-system-mastering-bjj-kimura-techniques-and-submissions))
- Opponent **straightens the arm to defend the kimura** -> straight-armlock / armbar.
- Opponent **stiff-arms / hugs their own beltline** -> slide top knee behind back, step through, seatbelt, peel arm -> **back take** (hooks / body triangle).
- Opponent **rolls away** -> keep figure-four, step over the head, thread knee behind shoulder -> reverse triangle while keeping the wrist.
- Counter to be aware of: opponent can hunt an **armbar** on you; defend by pushing their wrist to their belly and stopping their shoulder from posturing. ([RollBliss](https://rollbliss.com/blogs/news/kimura-trap-system-mastering-bjj-kimura-techniques-and-submissions))

### Chain D: Front Headlock — Guillotine <-> D'Arce <-> Anaconda <-> Back take
**[FLOW]** Verified primary source. "A grappler who knows which defensive reaction produces which transition presents a closed system with no clean escape." ([FighterIQ](https://fighteriq.substack.com/p/the-front-headlock-system-guillotine), [BJJ Fanatics](https://bjjfanatics.com/blogs/news/distinguishing-the-guillotine-darce-and-anaconda-chokes))
- Guillotine -> opponent **tucks chin, rotates head away, drives shoulder into your choking arm** -> their near arm goes deeper under your body and head turns away = exactly the D'Arce setup -> D'Arce. ([FighterIQ](https://fighteriq.substack.com/p/the-front-headlock-system-guillotine))
- D'Arce -> opponent **pulls the near arm out to block the thread** -> repositions arm into the anaconda grip -> anaconda (finish requires forward roll over their near shoulder, ending chest-up with them face-down). ([FighterIQ](https://fighteriq.substack.com/p/the-front-headlock-system-guillotine))
- D'Arce defended overall / arm comes free -> guillotine variations or **back take**. ([BJJ Fanatics](https://bjjfanatics.com/blogs/news/distinguishing-the-guillotine-darce-and-anaconda-chokes))
- Grip/condition key (game state flag): **arm IN your grip -> guillotine; near arm UNDER your body -> D'Arce; near arm threaded from far side over near arm -> anaconda.** Guillotine arm-out = blood choke; arm-in = trachea/crank. ([FighterIQ](https://fighteriq.substack.com/p/the-front-headlock-system-guillotine))

## Section 2 — Leg Lock System

### Position hierarchy (Danaher / DDS-derived)
Control before submission is the organizing law of the whole system. ([BJJ Graph Danaher System](https://bjjgraph.org/Systems/Danaher-Leg-Lock-System), [Evolve Ashi Garami](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/basic-ashi-garami-positions-explained/))
- **Ashi Garami (single-leg-X / SLX)** — foundational entanglement: one leg across the hip, other hooks behind the knee. Gateway to deeper controls; access to straight ankle lock, and entry to heel hooks. ([BJJ Graph](https://bjjgraph.org/Systems/Danaher-Leg-Lock-System), [Evolve](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/basic-ashi-garami-positions-explained/))
- **Inside Ashi-Garami** — higher control, dominant **inside heel hook** access. ([BJJ Graph](https://bjjgraph.org/Systems/Danaher-Leg-Lock-System))
- **Saddle / Honey Hole / Inside Sankaku / 411 / 80-20** — same position, many names. Both your legs between opponent's legs, inside knee threaded, triangle around the trapped leg. **Maximum control + direct line to the inside heel hook = highest-percentage finish in modern grappling.** ([BJJ Graph](https://bjjgraph.org/Systems/Danaher-Leg-Lock-System), [Living as a Grappler](https://livingasagrappler.substack.com/p/leg-lock-everyone-with-this-system))
- **Outside Ashi-Garami** — outside heel hook access; less torque/control than inside.
- **50/50** — symmetric leg interlock. Stalls under points rules, but a strong heel-hook finishing position in submission-only. ([Evolve](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/bjj-101-leg-locks/))
- Other listed control positions: Cross Ashi, Backside 50/50, Ushiro Ashi. ([BJJ Graph](https://bjjgraph.org/Systems/Danaher-Leg-Lock-System))

### Submissions
- **Inside heel hook** — heel rotates inward (toward midline). Most dangerous; torques MCL/ACL. Highest-percentage modern finish. ([Taipei BJJ](https://taipeibjj.com/heel-hook-bjj-attack-defend-submission/), [BJJInfo Heel Hook](https://bjjinfo.org/wiki/Heel_Hook))
- **Outside heel hook** — heel rotates outward; still dangerous, less structural torque. ([Taipei BJJ](https://taipeibjj.com/heel-hook-bjj-attack-defend-submission/))
- **Straight ankle lock (Achilles)** — beginner-legal entry-level; the only leg attack legal for IBJJF gi blue+ (see Section 9). ([Evolve](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/bjj-101-leg-locks/))
- **Kneebar** (hyperextends knee), **Toe hold** (rotational ankle/knee), **Estima lock**, **calf slicer**. ([BJJ Graph](https://bjjgraph.org/Systems/Danaher-Leg-Lock-System))

### Control-first principle (training emphasis -> game scaffolding)
- "Control first, torque second — every safe and successful heel hook follows that sequence." The best leg-lockers spend far more time on **positional control** (ashi, 50/50, saddle as positions) than on finishing mechanics. ([Evolve](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/basic-ashi-garami-positions-explained/), [BJJ Graph](https://bjjgraph.org/Systems/Danaher-Leg-Lock-System))

### Defense principles (the "right answers" when defending legs)
**[FLOW]**
- **Hide the heel** ("boot" the foot / pull heel to your own butt) so the attacker can't expose it. ([BJJInfo Heel Hook](https://bjjinfo.org/wiki/Heel_Hook), [Grapplearts heel hook safety](https://www.grapplearts.com/heel-hook-train-safely/))
- **Hand-fight** the heel-exposing grip before they secure it.
- **Rotate / spin in the SAME direction as the heel-hook torque** ("clear the knee line", roll toward the trouble) to unwind the lock before it loads.
- Recover the **knee line** (get your knee past their controlling leg) to escape the entanglement.
- Crucial safety note: because rotational knee locks can damage ligaments **before pain registers**, you must **tap to position/control, not wait for pain.** ([BJJ World](https://bjj-world.com/heel-hook-injury-knee-damage/), [BJJ Rehab](https://www.bjjrehab.com/techniques/heel-hook-injury))

### Modern meta + the Spanias finding (academic backbone for "position not always required")
- **Spanias, Kirk & Øvretveit (2022), "Position before submission? Techniques and tactics in competitive no-gi Brazilian jiu-jitsu", Revista de Artes Marciales Asiáticas 17(2):130-139.** Time-motion analysis, 52 athletes (26 regional + 26 international), light-feather to super-heavy, no-gi submission-only. ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/366435891_Position_before_submission_Techniques_and_tactics_in_competitive_no-gi_Brazilian_jiu-jitsu), [open PDF](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/31193/1/Spanias%20et%20al%202022%20BJJ%20Time%20Motion%20Analysis.pdf))
  - Positional dominance duration differed significantly between **winners vs losers**, and **correlated with upper-body submissions**.
  - **But positional dominance was inconsequential in matches decided by lower-body submissions** — athletes winning by lower-body submission (mostly heel hooks) "exerted no positional control prior to their victory." This is the academic basis for: **heel hooks can be hit without prior positional dominance**, unlike most upper-body subs. ([ResearchGate abstract via search](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/366435891_Position_before_submission_Techniques_and_tactics_in_competitive_no-gi_Brazilian_jiu-jitsu))
- **Game-design implication:** leg entanglements break the usual "position -> submission" tier rule. A leg-locker archetype can threaten a finish from neutral/scramble states, not only from dominant control. **[UNCERTAIN]** the stronger framing "needs *less* control than any other sub" is coaching consensus + Spanias's correlational data, not a controlled biomechanics proof.

### Legality (summary; full detail in Section 9)
- **IBJJF gi: heel hooks + reaping ILLEGAL at every belt.** ([BJJInfo Heel Hook Rules](https://bjjinfo.org/wiki/Heel_Hook_Rules_by_Organization), [FloGrappling](https://www.flograppling.com/articles/6856722-the-new-ibjjf-rules-for-heel-hooks-and-leg-reaping-in-2021))
- **IBJJF no-gi (since Jan 1 2021): heel hooks + reaping legal for adult BROWN/BLACK only.** Still illegal white/blue/purple and Masters. ([FloGrappling](https://www.flograppling.com/articles/6856722-the-new-ibjjf-rules-for-heel-hooks-and-leg-reaping-in-2021), [You Jiu Jitsu](https://youjiujitsu.com/heel-hooks-now-legal-in-ibjjf-no-gi/))
- **ADCC / EBI / submission-only: heel hooks legal for all (except beginner/amateur events).** ([BJJ Heroes rulesets](https://www.bjjheroes.com/bjj-culture/adcc-ebi-ibjjf-gpc-how-rules-change-jiu-jitsu))

## Section 3 — Wrestling for BJJ

### Takedowns (entries + finishes)
- **Double leg** — level change, penetration step, drive through; finish by running the pipe, lifting, or turning the corner. ([Grapplearts wrestling for BJJ](https://www.grapplearts.com/wrestling-moves-for-bjj/), [Evolve takedowns](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/wrestling-for-non-wrestlers-essential-takedown-strategies-for-mma-and-bjj-practitioners/))
- **Single leg** — high or low; head position is variant-dependent (inside vs outside the hip). Finishes: run the pipe, dump, trip. ([BJJ Fanatics single leg](https://bjjfanatics.com/blogs/news/single-leg-takedown-bjj), [Grapplearts](https://www.grapplearts.com/wrestling-moves-for-bjj/))
- **Ankle pick** — often off a collar tie / grip break; pick the ankle as you push the head past. Low risk of leg-lock counter. ([Evolve ankle pick](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/heres-how-to-utilize-ankle-picks-for-bjj/), [BJJ World](https://bjj-world.com/ankle-pick-takedown-bjj/))
- **Snap down -> front headlock** — snap the head down off pressure/reach; collapse them to all fours or expose the front headlock. Directly feeds Chain D (Section 1). ([Evolve snap down](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/bjj-takedown-101-the-snap-down/), [BJJEE](https://www.bjjee.com/articles/the-snap-down-setup-against-bad-wrestlers-in-jiu-jitsu/))
- **Arm drag -> back** — drag the arm across, circle to the back; works standing, seated, and from guard. ([Evolve arm drag](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/the-arm-drag-bjjs-silent-game-changer/), [BJJEE arm drag to back](https://www.bjjee.com/articles/heres-how-to-arm-drag-anyone-and-take-their-back/))

### Takedown defense
- **Sprawl** — kick legs back, drop hips/weight on their shoulders/head to defend the shot; feeds front headlock. ([Evolve counter takedowns](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/5-ways-to-counter-takedowns-in-no-gi-bjj/), [Grapplearts](https://www.grapplearts.com/wrestling-moves-for-bjj/))
- **Whizzer (over-hook)** — over-hook the shooting arm, drive down to deny the finish / counter to the side. ([Elite Sports whizzer](https://www.elitesports.com/blogs/news/what-is-a-whizzer-in-bjj), [BJJ Graph whizzer](https://bjjgraph.org/Principles/Whizzer-Control))
- **Down block / head position / hip movement** — frame the forearm on the thigh/shoulder, fight head inside, kick hips back. ([RollBliss TD defense](https://rollbliss.com/blogs/news/how-to-improve-your-takedown-defense-in-jiu-jitsu))
- **Granby roll** — shoulder roll to escape rides / re-face / clear hips when turtled or being controlled. ([Evolve granby](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/how-to-use-the-granby-roll-for-bjj/))

### Wrestle-up (bottom -> standing -> takedown)
- Coming up from seated/butterfly/SLX guard into a takedown. Used in BJJ to score from bottom, counter passers, and chain into leg locks. ([BJJEE wrestle-up](https://www.bjjee.com/articles/wrestle-up-the-unfair-advantage-in-bjj/), [Evolve wrestle-up + leg locks](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/heres-how-to-combine-wrestle-ups-with-leg-locks/))
- **[FLOW]** Wrestle-up: secure a leg/under-hook while seated -> opponent **postures back to defend** -> elevate/come up to single or body lock; opponent **drops weight to flatten you** -> switch to SLX/ashi -> leg lock. ([Evolve](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/heres-how-to-combine-wrestle-ups-with-leg-locks/))

### Turtle (protect the back)
- Turtle = defensive shell; primary goal is **protect the neck and prevent hooks/seatbelt**. Used after a stuffed shot or to deny back exposure while recovering. ([Evolve turtle guide](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/the-complete-guide-to-the-bjj-turtle-position/), [Tenace turtle](https://tenacesports.com/en/blogs/ndews/turtle-position-in-bjj-and-grappling))
- **Danger:** in BJJ, turtling invites back takes; you must immediately work to recover guard, granby, or stand. The same position is "safe" in folkstyle wrestling but exposing in BJJ. ([Evolve turtle guide](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/the-complete-guide-to-the-bjj-turtle-position/))

### Wrestling vs BJJ strategy difference (why the "right answer" differs)
- BJJ allows **guard pulling** (neutral in IBJJF, penalized in ADCC); a wrestler's reflex to scramble up can walk into leg entanglements; **back exposure** that's fine in wrestling is catastrophic in BJJ (seatbelt + hooks = losing position). Shooting a sloppy double exposes you to guillotines and leg locks. ([BJJ vs Wrestling](https://bjj-world.com/bjj-vs-wrestling/), [Simple BJJ pull-vs-takedown](https://simplebjj.com/pull-guard-or-takedown/), [RollBliss combine](https://rollbliss.com/blogs/news/how-to-effectively-combine-wrestling-takedowns-with-bjj))

## Section 4 — Guard Systems & Passing Systems

### Guards: what the guard player threatens vs what the passer should do
**[FLOW]** Each line = guard -> (player's threat) -> (passer's correct response).
- **Closed guard** -> threats: armbar/triangle/omoplata/kimura, hip-bump & scissor sweeps, guillotine -> passer: posture up, control hips, open the legs safely (knee-in-tailbone / log split), then pass. ([Evolve closed guard attacks](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/5-attacks-from-the-bjj-full-guard/), [BJJ Fanatics defending closed guard](https://bjjfanatics.com/blogs/news/defending-submissions-from-inside-the-closed-guard-101))
- **Half guard / knee shield (Z-guard)** -> threats: sweeps, back take, knee-shield kimura, leg-lock entries -> passer: kill the knee shield (cross-face + under-hook battle), flatten them, free the trapped leg. ([BJJ Graph half guard](https://bjjgraph.org/Positions/Half-Guard), [Evolve Z-guard](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/complete-guide-to-the-bjj-z-guard/), [Galvao kill knee shield](https://bjjfanatics.com/products/killing-the-knee-shield-half-guard-by-andre-galvao))
- **Deep half** -> threats: sweeps from under the hips -> passer: whizzer + fight to back/flatten, don't get rolled. ([Evolve deep half](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/bjj-101-deep-half-guard/))
- **De La Riva (DLR)** -> threats: sweeps, berimbolo to back, back take -> passer: clear the DLR hook, get to **Headquarters (HQ)**, free leg between yours, squat heavy. ([Evolve DLR](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/what-is-the-de-la-riva-guard/), [Evolve pass DLR](https://evolve-university.com/blog/how-to-pass-the-de-la-riva-guard-in-bjj/))
- **Reverse DLR (RDLR)** -> threats: spin-under, berimbolo, leg drag of you, back take -> passer: push the hook down, switch wrist->ankle grip, drop knee on the calf. ([Evolve RDLR](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/what-is-the-reverse-de-la-riva-guard-in-bjj/), [Danaher RDLR pass](https://bjjfanatics.com/blogs/news/john-danaher-s-brilliant-reverse-de-la-riva-pass))
- **Spider guard** -> threats: sweeps, triangle/omoplata, off-balancing via sleeve+foot tension -> passer: break the grips, bend the controlled arm, pin the feet to the mat. ([BJJ Fanatics spider](https://bjjfanatics.com/blogs/news/spider-guard-bjj))
- **Lasso guard** -> threats: omoplata, triangle, sweeps -> passer: free the lassoed arm (circle out / leg-lasso pass), step around. ([Evolve lasso](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/complete-guide-to-the-bjj-lasso-guard/), [Grapplearts lasso pass](https://www.grapplearts.com/leg-lasso-spider-guard-pass/))
- **Butterfly guard** -> threats: butterfly/hook sweeps, elevation, wrestle-up -> passer: drive a knee through to the floor, smash hips to the side, kill the hooks. ([Evolve pass butterfly](https://evolve-university.com/blog/how-to-pass-the-butterfly-guard-in-bjj/))
- **X-guard / single-leg-X (SLX)** -> threats: sweeps, leg locks (ashi entries) -> passer: free the trapped leg, posture, keep base low. ([NAGA X-guard sweep](https://www.nagafighter.com/what-is-the-x-guard-sweep-in-bjj/), [BJJ Graph SLX](https://bjjgraph.org/Positions/X-Guard/Single-Leg-X-Guard))
- **Berimbolo** = inversion from DLR (often) to spin underneath and **take the back** (often into the crab-ride/leg-drag-back). Defense: clear the hook early, stay heavy, deny the inversion. ([Evolve berimbolo](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/when-and-how-should-i-use-the-berimbolo-in-bjj/), [Evolve defend berimbolo](https://evolve-university.com/blog/how-to-defend-against-the-bjj-berimbolo/))

### Passing styles
- **Pressure passing** — knee cut/knee slice, over-under, body lock, stack, smash. Slow, heavy, control-based. ([Evolve speed vs pressure](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/speed-passing-vs-pressure-passing-in-bjj/))
- **Speed / movement passing** — toreando (bullfighter), leg drag, long step, X-pass, floating. Fast, footwork-based. ([Evolve speed vs pressure](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/speed-passing-vs-pressure-passing-in-bjj/))
- **Reality:** top players hybridize, chaining speed->pressure off reactions ("take what they give"). **[UNCERTAIN]** the clean speed/pressure buckets are a teaching simplification; knee cut and leg drag are often called hybrid connectors. ([Evolve](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/speed-passing-vs-pressure-passing-in-bjj/), [JiuJitsu News 2025 meta](https://jiujitsu-news.com/top-bjj-guard-passes/))

### Passing if-then chains
**[FLOW]**
- Read the legs: legs low/flat -> toreando or knee cut; feet at chest -> force feet down then speed pass; feet high -> stack/pressure pass. ([Evolve](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/speed-passing-vs-pressure-passing-in-bjj/))
- **Knee cut requirement:** you NEED a far-side under-hook — without it "they will take your back." ([BJJ Graph knee cut pass](https://bjjgraph.org/Transitions/Knee-Cut-Pass))
- Knee cut -> opponent **frames on hip/shoulder** -> secure under-hook before frames extend, keep forward pressure; if they **frame hard** -> backstep to leg drag; if they **insert knee shield** -> over-under or leg drag. ([BJJ Graph](https://bjjgraph.org/Transitions/Knee-Cut-Pass), [Evolve knee slice](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/how-to-perform-the-knee-slice-cut-pass-in-bjj/))
- Toreando -> opponent **shrimps/recovers** -> do NOT reset to neutral (they regain closed guard); chain toreando -> smash pass; if they shrimp again -> stack or leg drag. ([Evolve counter toreando](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/heres-the-most-effective-way-to-counter-the-toreando-pass/))
- **Leg drag = the connector** between speed and pressure; from it reach side control, back, mount, or armbar. ([Evolve leg drag](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/how-to-perform-the-leg-drag-guard-pass/), [Grapplearts leg drag](https://www.grapplearts.com/how-to-do-the-leg-drag-guard-pass/))
  - Leg drag -> opponent **stays still** -> mount; **turns/rolls to turtle** -> insert hook + seatbelt -> take the back; **flattens back to mat** -> side control. ([HubPages leg drag to back](https://discover.hubpages.com/sports/Leg-Drag-to-Back-Take-a-BJJ-Tutorial), [BJJ World](https://bjj-world.com/leg-drag-pass-masterclass/))
- Body lock pass control points: pin hips-to-hips (kills space/frames), shoulder-on-jaw to flatten shoulders, stay low so elbows control hips, then walk the legs by. ([BJJ Fanatics body lock](https://bjjfanatics.com/blogs/news/the-5-stages-of-the-body-lock-pass), [Evolve body lock](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/everything-you-need-to-know-about-the-body-lock-in-bjj-and-mma/))

## Section 5 — Escape Systems

### Timing principle (the master cue for the game)
- Escape **before the position consolidates**: begin the moment their knee crosses your centerline / before the cross-face and under-hook lock in. Late escapes fail against a settled top player. ([Evolve 10 escapes](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/10-fundamental-bjj-escapes-all-grapplers-should-know/), [BJJ Graph side control escapes](https://bjjgraph.org/Principles/Side-Control-Escapes))

### Mount escapes
**[FLOW]**
- **Trap-and-roll (upa/bridge):** trap an arm + same-side foot, bridge over that shoulder. Opponent **bases out the trapped-side arm** -> the roll fails, switch to elbow-knee escape. ([Grapplearts upa errors](https://www.grapplearts.com/the-4-most-common-errors-for-the-upa-mount-escape/))
- **Elbow-knee (knee-elbow escape):** frame, shrimp, insert knee -> recover half guard / knee shield. Opponent **drives weight forward to re-mount / grapevines** -> switch back to bridge (upa). The two escapes are a pair: bridge forces a base, base opens the shrimp. ([Evolve 10 escapes](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/10-fundamental-bjj-escapes-all-grapplers-should-know/))

### Side control escapes
**[FLOW]**
- **Frame + shrimp** to recover guard. Opponent **re-pressures the cross-face** -> re-frame on the neck/hip and shrimp again, or switch to ghost. ([Evolve 3 side control escapes](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/3-escapes-from-the-side-control-in-bjj/), [BJJ Graph](https://bjjgraph.org/Principles/Side-Control-Escapes))
- **Ghost / running escape:** spin away under their chest when they **over-commit pressure** -> recover guard or come up. ([Elite Sports ghost escape](https://www.elitesports.com/blogs/news/bjj-ghost-escape-best-way-to-escape-side-control))
- **Under-hook to come up:** get the near under-hook, come to a seated position / wrestle-up when they **lighten pressure to attack**. ([Evolve 3 escapes](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/3-escapes-from-the-side-control-in-bjj/))

### Back escapes
**[FLOW]** Priority order: **protect the neck -> fall to the choke side -> scoot hips down -> clear the hooks -> recover.** ([BJJ World back escapes](https://bjj-world.com/bjj-back-escapes-counters/), [Sensei Ando / Kesting RNC defense](https://www.senseiando.com/rear-naked-choke-defense/))
- **Two-on-one** wrist control on the choking arm prevents the RNC while you escape. ([BJJ Graph hand-fighting from back](https://bjjgraph.org/Principles/Hand-Fighting-from-Back))
- Slide your back to the mat toward the **choking-arm side** (so you don't turn into the choke), then scoot hips below their hooks, clear one hook -> recover to side/guard. **[UNCERTAIN]** which side to fall (under-hook side vs choke-arm side) is system-dependent; both appear in reputable instruction. ([BJJ World](https://bjj-world.com/bjj-back-escapes-counters/))

### Knee-on-belly & North-South escapes
- **KOB:** frame on the knee, turn to your side, recover guard or come up; escape **before** they switch to mount/submission. ([BJJ Spot KOB](https://www.bjj-spot.com/knee-on-belly-attacks-escapes/), [Jiu Jitsu Legacy KOB](https://jiujitsulegacy.com/videos/techniques/knee-on-belly-escapes/))
- **North-South:** frame on the hips, bridge and turn to recover guard / take the back as they walk around.

## Section 6 — Opponent Styles / Archetypes (for enemy AI design)

For each: signature attack pattern -> how the player should game-plan against it. **[FLOW]** = the counter strategy differs per archetype.

- **Pressure Passer** — chest-to-chest, knee cut / over-under / body lock, heavy smash. Counter: strong frames + early hip movement, knee shield / half guard, recover guard before they flatten you; don't let them establish the cross-face. ([Evolve passers vs players](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/guard-passers-vs-guard-players-a-guide-to-picking-your-bjj-style/), [Evolve speed vs pressure](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/speed-passing-vs-pressure-passing-in-bjj/))
- **Leg-Locker (modern ashi specialist / "ashi-garami otoko")** — hunts entanglements (SLX, saddle, 50/50) from any scramble; per Spanias, can finish **without prior positional dominance**. Counter: hide the heel, hand-fight the entry, never give a straight panicked leg, rotate with the lock, recover the knee line; be cautious wrestling-up into their game. ([Evolve leg locks legality](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/where-and-when-are-leg-locks-allowed-in-bjj/), [BJJ World leg-lock counters to single leg](https://bjj-world.com/leg-locks-counters-single-leg/), Spanias 2022)
- **Guard Player** — pulls guard, plays DLR/spider/lasso/butterfly, sweeps & submission chains. Counter: posture and grip-fight, pass with pressure to deny their dynamic open guards, don't engage in their grip game on their terms. ([Evolve passers vs players](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/guard-passers-vs-guard-players-a-guide-to-picking-your-bjj-style/))
- **Wrestler / takedown-heavy** — chain takedowns (double/single/ankle pick), top pressure, scramble. Counter: takedown defense (sprawl, whizzer, down block), threaten guillotine/front headlock/leg locks on their shots, pull guard to neutralize standup if rules allow. ([Evolve counter takedowns](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/5-ways-to-counter-takedowns-in-no-gi-bjj/), [BJJ vs Wrestling](https://bjj-world.com/bjj-vs-wrestling/))
- **Judoka (gi)** — dominant grips + throws (osoto, uchi mata, seoi); strong standing. Counter: deny grips / break grips early, change levels, drop to guard to take away the throw, attack on the landing. ([BJJEE throw defensive opponents](https://www.bjjee.com/articles/how-to-throw-ultra-defensive-opponents-in-judo-bjj/))
- **Submission / choke hunter** — constantly threatens chokes (RNC, guillotine, triangle, D'Arce). Counter: posture and hand-fight, protect the neck, don't give the head/arm combination, escape early before the position consolidates. ([BJJEE BJJ styles](https://www.bjjee.com/articles/understanding-various-bjj-styles-pressure-dynamic-half-guard-2/))

## Section 7 — Coaching / Motor Learning (teaching-game design implications)

- **Constraints-Led Approach (CLA) / Ecological Dynamics:** manipulate task constraints (rules, space, grips, scoring) so the correct solution *emerges* through guided self-discovery rather than rote step memorization. Highly suited to grappling's chaotic, reactive nature. ([The Jiu Jitsu Lounge CLA](https://www.thejiujitsulounge.com/post/the-constraint-led-approach-in-jiu-jitsu-why-ecological-training-is-the-future-of-grappling), [Heavy BJJ task-based class](https://heavybjj.com/what-is-the-ecological-approach-to-jiu-jitsu-how-to-structure-a-task-based-bjj-class/), [Ecological dynamics & motor learning design (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/236668224_Ecological_dynamics_and_motor_learning_design_in_sport))
- **Positional sparring** = constrained live practice from a defined start (e.g., escape side control vs resisting partner). NOTE one source argues CLA != positional sparring; CLA is broader (task/constraint design), positional sparring is one tool. **[UNCERTAIN]** terminology debated. ([BJJ Insights](https://bjjinsights.com/ecological-approach-bjj/), [Ground Game Theory](https://www.groundgametheory.com/blog/the-case-for-the-ecological-approach/))
- **Internal vs external focus of attention:** external focus (on the effect/environment/opponent) generally beats internal focus (on your own body parts) for motor learning and execution; some evidence supports internal focus during *preparation* and external during *execution*. ([Wulf et al. internal/external focus](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20037032/), [Eur J Sport Sci 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17461391.2022.2042604))
- **Differential learning:** deliberately add movement variability/noise instead of repeating one "ideal" rep. Promising but **[UNCERTAIN]** / contested in the literature. ([Differential learning chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-98787-8_2), [Frontiers Psychology](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.533033/full))
- **Game-design takeaways:**
  - Use **representative tasks** (realistic positions + a resisting reaction), not isolated drills.
  - Build **variability** into opponent reactions so the player learns to read, not memorize.
  - **Scaffold difficulty** via constraints (limit the opponent's options early, expand later).
  - Frame feedback toward the **external effect** ("break their posture", "clear the knee line") rather than internal body cues.

## Section 8 — Safety / Injury Epidemiology

### Scoggin et al. 2014 (verified directly) — competition injuries
"Assessment of Injuries During Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu Competition", Orthopaedic Journal of Sports Medicine. ([PMC4555620](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4555620/))
- 2,511 matches = 5,022 athlete-exposures, 8 Hawaii tournaments 2005-2011; 46 injuries.
- **Overall rate: 9.2 injuries / 1,000 exposures** — lower than judo, taekwondo, wrestling, MMA.
- Orthopedic injuries = 78% of all; costochondral/rib 15.2%; lacerations 6.5%.
- **Elbow most injured: 38.9% of orthopedic injuries (14 cases). Knee second: 19.4% (7).**
- **Armbar caused 10 of 14 elbow injuries** -> authors propose the "jiu-jitsu arm bar" as a recognized hyperextension mechanism. Game-relevant: armbar = the canonical "elbow danger" submission.
- Age: <=15 yrs = 4.1/1,000 vs 16+ = 10.8/1,000.

### Heel hooks / knee — Piekarski et al. 2026 (Sports Health) + case reports
- Modern competition data: **heel hooks associated with markedly elevated knee-injury risk** (the bjjdoc summary cites a "12-fold" figure). Inside heel hook torques ACL/MCL/meniscus. ([Piekarski 2026 PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12815628/), [bjjdoc summary](https://bjjdoc.com/2026/01/31/heel-hooks-increase-knee-injury-risk-12-fold-in-bjj-competitions-new-study-finds/))
- **Pain-delay danger:** rotational knee locks can damage ligaments **before pain registers** -> hence "tap to position/control, not to pain", and slow/no-cranking application. ([ACL rupture case report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19629437/), [BJJ World](https://bjj-world.com/heel-hook-injury-knee-damage/))
- **[UNCERTAIN]** the "Spanias 2022" reference in the original brief is actually the *tactical* paper (Section 2), not a biomechanics/injury paper; it supports "heel hooks finish without positional dominance", not the injury mechanism. The injury/pain-delay claims come from Piekarski 2026 + case reports + coaching sources.

### Neck / cervical
- Mechanisms: stacking, neck cranks, guillotines, forced cervical flexion/rotation. In Scoggin competition data, cervical injuries were rare (1 cervical strain from a triangle) — but that's *competition* exposure, not chronic training load. ([Scoggin PMC4555620](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4555620/))

### Tapping culture
- "Tap early" as a *measured* intervention isn't quantified in the literature found; it's coaching consensus reinforced by the heel-hook/neck-crank pain-delay reality. ([BJJ World](https://bjj-world.com/heel-hook-injury-knee-damage/))
- **Denominator caution:** competition per-1,000-AE rates vs per-athlete-year training rates are NOT comparable — don't mix them in game stats/UI. ([BJJEE lowest injury rate](https://www.bjjee.com/articles/new-study-jiu-jitsu-competitors-have-the-lowest-injury-rate-among-combat-athletes/))

## Section 9 — Belt Curriculum & Ruleset Differences (difficulty design + why "correct" changes)

### Learning order (difficulty-scaffolding reference)
- **Gracie Combatives (36 techniques):** 36 core techniques (70+ variations), street-defense focused, 23 one-hour classes, each lesson pairs one standing + one ground technique. Completion (+ Reflex Development + 25-min test) -> Gracie Blue Belt + Master Cycle. Blue Belt Stripe 1 then teaches **counters** to the Combatives. -> natural tier model: base technique tier, then a "counter" tier. ([Gracie University course](https://www.gracieuniversity.com/Pages/Public/Course?se=58), [Ronin Athletics](https://roninathletics.com/blog/the-gracie-approach-to-teaching-jiu-jitsu-part-2-of-2-the-curriculum-and-a-new-way-of-sparring))
- **Gracie Barra GB1 (Fundamentals):** 16-week rotating cycle, ~96 core techniques/concepts. **Early = safe falling + core positions + survival/escapes FIRST; submissions and advanced/modern guards come later.** ([GB Northridge](https://gbnorthridge.com/the-gracie-barra-method-a-structured-curriculum-for-all-levels/), [BJJ World GB curriculum](https://bjj-world.com/gracie-barra-jiu-jitsu-curriculum/))
- **General difficulty arc:** survival/escapes (early tiers) -> submissions -> sweeps -> advanced/modern guards -> leg entanglements (gated late). Maps cleanly to game tiers: **escapes = beginner; leg-lock entanglements = advanced.** ([Grapplearts IBJJF implications](https://www.grapplearts.com/implications-of-the-ibjjf-legalising-heel-hooks-and-reaping/))

- Omoplata | hides/straightens arm -> Armbar or Back take
- Closed guard | postures back far -> Hip-bump sweep
- Hip-bump | posts the hand -> Kimura
- Kimura | hides hand/stiff-arms -> Omoplata
- Kimura | head forces arm back inside -> Guillotine

**Kimura trap (S1):**
- Kimura grip | straightens arm -> Armbar/straight-armlock
- Kimura grip | hugs beltline -> Back take
- Kimura grip | rolls away -> Reverse triangle

**Front headlock (S1):**
- Guillotine | tucks chin + turns away + shoulder-in -> D'Arce
- D'Arce | pulls near arm out -> Anaconda
- D'Arce | arm comes free -> Guillotine or Back take
- (state flag) arm IN -> Guillotine; near arm UNDER you -> D'Arce; arm over from far side -> Anaconda

**Passing (S4):**
- Read legs: flat -> Toreando/Knee-cut; feet at chest -> force down + speed pass; feet high -> Stack/pressure
- Knee cut | frames on hip -> secure under-hook (mandatory) / if hard frame -> Leg drag
- Knee cut | inserts knee shield -> Over-under or Leg drag
- Toreando | shrimps/recovers -> Smash pass (don't reset); shrimps again -> Stack/Leg drag
- Leg drag | stays still -> Mount; turns to turtle -> hook+seatbelt Back take; flattens -> Side control
- DLR | threatens berimbolo -> clear hook to HQ
- RDLR | tries to spin -> push hook down, wrist->ankle, knee on calf
- Butterfly | sits up to elevate -> knee through to floor + smash hips

**Wrestling (S3):**
- Shot incoming -> Sprawl / Whizzer / Down block (-> front headlock)
- Wrestle-up | postures back -> come up to single/body lock
- Wrestle-up | drops weight to flatten -> SLX/ashi -> leg lock
- Stuffed their shot -> Front headlock chain (S1 Chain D)

**Escapes (S5) — start the moment knee crosses centerline / before cross-face:**
- Mount upa | bases trapped-side arm -> switch to elbow-knee escape
- Mount elbow-knee | drives forward to re-mount -> switch back to upa
- Side control | re-pressures cross-face -> re-frame + shrimp, or Ghost escape
- Side control | over-commits pressure -> Ghost/running escape
- Back | choke threatens -> two-on-one wrist control, fall to choke side, scoot hips, clear hook
- KOB | settles -> frame knee, turn, recover before mount/sub

**Leg defense (S2) — tap to control, not pain:**
- Entangled | they expose heel -> hide the heel ("boot"), hand-fight grip
- Heel hook loading -> rotate with the torque + recover knee line

**Archetype counters (S6):**
- vs Pressure passer -> frames + early hips + knee shield
- vs Leg-locker -> hide heel, hand-fight entry, cautious wrestle-up
- vs Guard player -> posture + grip-fight + pressure pass
- vs Wrestler -> sprawl/whizzer + guillotine/leglock on shots
- vs Judoka -> break grips early, change level, drop to guard
- vs Choke hunter -> posture, protect neck, escape early
